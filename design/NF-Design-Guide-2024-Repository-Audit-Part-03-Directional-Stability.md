# NF Design Guide 2024 — Salamandra Repository Audit

**Part 3: lateral-directional stability, adverse yaw, finite sideslip, stall,
launch and landing control**  
**Audit date:** 20 August 2026  
**Aircraft baseline:** Salamandra Article #1, release v0.6.0  
**Source:** Peter Wick, *Designing Flying Wings* (2024 English PDF), principally PDF
pages 70–120, 151–169, 199–212 and 270–303  
**Previous:** [Part 2 — airfoil fidelity, pitch trim, static margin and elevon authority](NF-Design-Guide-2024-Repository-Audit-Part-02-Airfoil-Trim.md)
**Next:** [Part 4 — printed structure, joints and aeroelastic release](NF-Design-Guide-2024-Repository-Audit-Part-04-Structure-Aeroelasticity.md)

## 1. Part 3 disposition

**The twin-fin V1 architecture is a rational and reversible test configuration, but its
current analysis does not establish safe lateral-directional behavior for first flight.**

The repository deserves credit for publishing the CLEAN configuration as directionally
unstable, carrying independent uncertainty corners for V1 static stability, refusing to
credit unmeasured propeller slipstream and marking the twin fins as provisional. Those are
sound engineering decisions.

The apparent dynamic closure is narrower than the documentation suggests. The published
V1 mode, `−0.952 ± 5.239i /s`, uses only the nominal `Cnβ = +0.0005/deg`. Propagating the
already-published V1 lower corner, `Cnβ = −0.000290/deg`, through the same reduced model
produces a divergent real mode:

| Airspeed | Lower-corner dominant root | Approximate divergence time constant |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 45 km/h | `+0.983 /s` | 1.02 s |
| 60 km/h | `+1.614 /s` | 0.62 s |
| 95 km/h | `+3.010 /s` | 0.33 s |

These are diagnostic results `[I]`, not predictions of the physical aircraft. They prove a
narrower point: **the current model cannot simultaneously claim a stable V1 mode and retain
its published uncertainty band.** The uncertain static derivative must be propagated into
the dynamics, not replaced by its nominal value.

The model also lacks the mechanisms the NF guide identifies as decisive:

- a defined and manufactured fin airfoil with low-Reynolds-number side-force data;
- deadband, nonlinear `CY(beta)` and fin stall at finite sideslip;
- body/wing/fin interference and powered-wake effects;
- roll rate, bank angle, dihedral effect and roll–yaw coupling;
- geometry-derived fin `Cyβ` and `Cyr` in the state matrix;
- control-induced yaw from differential elevons;
- asymmetric stall progression in sideslip; and
- a yaw-aware launch, approach and landing envelope.

Accordingly:

| Item | Part 3 disposition |
|---|---|
| CLEAN as the O1 low-drag research configuration | **Retain; not a first-flight configuration** |
| V1 twin-fin architecture | **Retain as provisional and reversible** |
| V1a geometry as flight-released directional solution | **Not demonstrated** |
| Published nominal V1 reduced mode | **Retain only as one sensitivity point** |
| Claim that E8 yaw decay alone will close G10 | **Withdraw; identification scope is insufficient** |
| No-rudder architecture | **May remain provisional; rationale must be mission- and failure-mode-based** |
| Two-degree polyhedral schedule | **Retain as an unvalidated design variable** |
| First flight | **Blocked by the Part 1–3 P0 findings** |

## 2. Evidence and reproducibility

Numerical results in this part are generated by
[`calculations/nf_design_guide_part3_directional_stability.py`](../calculations/nf_design_guide_part3_directional_stability.py):

```bash
python3 -m pip install -r calculations/requirements.txt
python3 calculations/nf_design_guide_part3_directional_stability.py
```

The script imports the current geometry, mass, CG, inertia, VLM and directional-stability
owners. It does not copy the released values into a separate model. Its outputs are
classified as follows:

- `[D]`: deterministic consequence of the current repository inputs;
- `[E]`: current engineering estimate or empirical band inherited from the repository;
- `[I]`: audit sensitivity or incomplete model, not a flight prediction; and
- `[M]`: physical measurement; no new `[M]` evidence exists in this part.

The evidence chain currently implemented by the repository is:

```text
estimated body side area and body factor
             +
estimated forward-swept-wing Cnβ band
             +
Helmbold fin CLα × low-Re factor × area × arm
             │
             ▼
      small-angle static Cnβ band
             │
             ├── select nominal +0.0005/deg only
             ├── estimate Cnr from wing and fin
             ├── insert generic Cyβ and Cyr
             ▼
      reduced two-state model (β, r)
             │
             ▼
        one nominal modal pair
```

The missing physical chain is:

```text
measured body + printed fin contour + gap/root/wake installation
             │
             ▼
CY, Cl and Cn versus β, p, r, alpha, control, Re and throttle
             │
             ▼
coupled lateral-directional model (β, p, r, φ and controller states)
             │
             ▼
launch / manoeuvre / approach / disturbance envelopes
             │
             ▼
instrumented identification and model correlation
```

## 3. What the NF guide contributes

### 3.1 Static directional stability and yaw damping are different requirements

The guide separates vertical-tail volume from dynamic damping. Fin area and longitudinal
arm both contribute linearly to small-angle static restoring moment, while the arm enters
yaw damping approximately squared (PDF pp. 98–100).

This matters directly to Salamandra because the pusher-propeller clearance forces the V1
fin aerodynamic centre to only 209.3 mm aft of the CG. A first-order sensitivity using the
repository's own equations gives:

| Change | Fin `Cnβ` | Fin `Cnr` |
|---|---:|---:|
| Current geometry | 1.000× | 1.000× |
| 20% longer arm, same area | 1.200× | 1.440× |
| 20% longer arm, area reduced by 1/1.20 | 1.000× | 1.200× |

This does not imply that propulsion should be moved or that a longer carrier is acceptable;
the repository has already shown serious packaging and CG consequences. It demonstrates
why matching one static `Cnβ` target does not make different fin locations dynamically
equivalent.

### 3.2 Fin planform efficiency depends on span loading, not area alone

The guide compares equal-area vertical tails and reports much better usable side force
when the span loading is more nearly uniform. A strongly tapered, low-aspect-ratio fin can
reach its local tip limit before using the potential of its root area. Higher aspect ratio
can improve slope and induced drag, but may also make the whole surface stall more abruptly
(PDF pp. 100–104).

These examples are not universal design ratios. Their transferable requirement is to
calculate the actual fin load distribution and nonlinear limit. Salamandra currently uses
`AR_v = 2.0`, taper 0.45 and one scalar Helmbold lift slope. It has no fin lattice, section
polar or spanwise stall analysis. The planform is structurally and geometrically defined,
but its aerodynamic utilization is not.

### 3.3 Low-Re vertical-tail sections can have a directional deadband

The guide shows measured low-Re behavior in which a symmetric section produces little or
no lift-slope response around zero angle. It attributes this to interacting laminar
separation bubbles and recommends a thin section selected specifically for the local
Reynolds-number range (PDF pp. 106–109).

The V1 operating regime is severe:

| Airspeed | Fin root Re | Fin MAC Re | Fin tip Re |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 45 km/h | 142,500 | 108,200 | 64,100 |
| 60 km/h | 189,900 | 144,300 | 85,500 |
| 75 km/h | 237,400 | 180,400 | 106,800 |
| 95 km/h | 300,700 | 228,500 | 135,300 |

The current `cla_fin_band()` comment describes approximately Re 150k–300k, but much of
the actual fin lies below that range during launch and landing. At 45 km/h the tip is only
about Re 64k.

The design guide calls the V1 section a “symmetric biconvex plate,” 3.0 mm thick at the
root and 1.5 mm at the tip, with an external 3 mm leading-edge rod. This gives plate
thickness ratios of only 1.755% and 1.950%. No coordinate file defines how the rod, plate,
maximum thickness and 0.8 mm trailing edge form a continuous aerodynamic contour. Thus
there is not yet a section that can be analyzed, manufactured and inspected consistently.

### 3.4 Small-angle `Cnβ` does not demonstrate finite-sideslip recovery

The guide's flight experience warns that an aircraft with a very small fin may be flyable
after a straight launch yet tumble after a modest yawed release. It also explains that a
highly efficient fin can lose most of its side force when the complete surface stalls
(PDF pp. 98–103).

The current Salamandra crosswind calculation reaches `beta = 24.0°` at 45 km/h forward
speed and 20 km/h crosswind. Extending the assumed linear fin slope to that point implies
`CL_fin = 0.90…1.06`. No V1 polar demonstrates this lift, and the lowest local Reynolds
number is about 64k. Therefore the reported `22.7°` hypothetical rudder demand is not a
validated control-authority result. It is the ratio of two linear derivatives evaluated
well outside their demonstrated range.

The proper finite-yaw question is broader:

1. Does the printed fin produce restoring side force without a deadband?
2. At what `beta`, Reynolds number and throttle does either fin separate?
3. Does the root/wing junction separate before the free fin?
4. How does asymmetric separation affect roll and pitch as well as yaw?
5. Can the aircraft return to the approved envelope with the available effectors?

### 3.5 Forward body side area must be treated as part of the aircraft

The guide recommends minimizing fuselage area ahead of the CG because it counteracts a
short-coupled vertical tail. It also emphasizes smooth wing/body transitions and avoiding
constrictions that provoke separation (PDF pp. 93–97).

Salamandra's long nose boom is required for battery balance, so the destabilizing mechanism
cannot simply be removed. The repository correctly includes a body `Cnβ` band, but the
input is still a generic `S_fs = 0.040 m²` with a broad body factor. The final body OML is
provisional and no finite-yaw flow field includes the nose, wing, motor pod, twin-fin roots
or propeller.

There is also an internal propagation inconsistency. The published CLEAN band holds
`S_fs` at its nominal value while varying the body factor:

| CLEAN screen | `Cnβ` range |
|---|---:|
| Published calculation | −0.001414…−0.000547 /deg |
| Independent corners of the declared `S_fs` and body-factor bands | −0.001674…−0.000437 /deg |

Both remain negative, so the architectural conclusion is unchanged. The difference shows
that uncertainty treatment is not yet uniform between CLEAN and V1.

### 3.6 Sweep creates coupled roll and yaw effects that vary with lift

The guide explains that aft sweep normally contributes a dihedral effect and induced-drag
directional restoring effect, both of which weaken at low lift. It explicitly notes that
forward sweep reverses these tendencies and normally requires compensating vertical area
and dihedral (PDF pp. 114–120).

The repository recognizes this qualitatively and specifies a provisional 2° piecewise
polyhedral assembly. Nevertheless:

- the aerodynamic VLM remains planar;
- no `Clβ` is calculated;
- the polyhedral schedule is absent from the yaw model;
- roll rate and bank angle are absent from the two-state yaw model; and
- no full spiral or Dutch-roll mode exists.

The current result is correctly described in some documents as a reduced `beta-r` pair.
It must not be used as a full lateral-directional stability assessment.

### 3.7 Adverse yaw is an operating-point and control-allocation problem

The guide explains that control-induced yaw changes with lift coefficient, aileron region,
lift distribution and flap mixing. It distinguishes adverse yaw during control application
from passive directional stability after the controls are neutralized (PDF pp. 159–169
and 199–211).

Salamandra calculates `Cl_delta_a` and `Cl_p` for the elevons. Its yaw module contains only
a first-order differential-elevon estimate of approximately `0.000080/deg`; the value is
not generated from the VLM load and drag change. There is no `Cn_delta_a(alpha, delta)`,
`Cy_delta_a`, asymmetric separation model or combined elevon/control-law simulation.

This invalidates the current description of E8 as a “rudder-kick analog via aileron
impulse.” During the forced interval, the input simultaneously excites roll, yaw and
possibly pitch. Without identifying that input matrix, a decay trace cannot separate
`Cn_delta_a` from `Cnβ`, `Cnr`, `Clβ` or the controller response.

### 3.8 Symmetric outer-wing reserve is necessary but not sufficient

The guide repeatedly requires lift reserve on the outer wing, especially in yaw, because
local separation can create strong roll/yaw coupling and an unrecoverable state. Its
winglet discussion is directly transferable as a warning about any loaded vertical-surface
junction, even though Salamandra correctly rejected wingtip fins for its forward-swept
planform (PDF pp. 273–301).

The current symmetric 80×8 VLM screen at 45 km/h gives:

| Semispan station | Local `Cl` | Reserve to generic `Cl = 0.65` |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.506 | 0.6287 | 0.0213 |
| 0.664 | 0.6285 | 0.0215 |
| 0.797 | 0.5919 | 0.0581 |
| 0.900 | 0.4992 | 0.1508 |
| 0.945 | 0.4096 | 0.2404 |

This is a genuine strength: the ideal symmetric model contains substantial outer-wing
reserve. The local peak is `Cl = 0.6320` near 60.3% semispan, close to the current generic
section limit. It does not prove the physical stall sequence because:

- `Clmax = 0.65` is not a local measured printed-section property;
- airfoil Reynolds number and surface condition vary along the span;
- the model contains no sideslip or differential-elevon command;
- the fin/root junction is absent; and
- VLM contains no separation physics.

### 3.9 Launch and landing must be designed as coupled transient states

The guide recommends a deliberate launch at adequate speed, precise CG, play-free
linkages, conservative initial control throws and progressive adjustment. It warns that a
weak launch can place a tailless aircraft in an oscillatory high-drag state before the
controls become effective (PDF pp. 151–153). For low-directional-stability configurations,
it further warns against yaw-constraining launch arrangements that prevent the roll needed
to follow the desired path (PDF pp. 209–212).

The Salamandra launch calculation is one-dimensional. It propagates forward speed with
thrust and drag and applies a scalar torque-roll threshold, but contains no:

- release yaw or roll angle;
- lateral hand impulse;
- crosswind/gust vector;
- `Cnβ`, `Clβ`, `Cn_delta_a` or control-system dynamics;
- fin stall or body shielding; or
- coupled pilot/autolaunch recovery logic.

Landing is even less defined. The book discusses split flaps, drag devices and controlled
stall-based landing modes, while emphasizing their pitch and flutter consequences (PDF
pp. 70–92). Salamandra has no released approach speed, flare authority, sink-rate limit,
touchdown-energy limit, wind envelope, braking mode or landing-area requirement. No brake
should be added merely because the book contains one; the missing step is to define the
landing requirement and prove whether the existing elevons satisfy it.

## 4. What the repository already does well

### 4.1 It does not conceal the CLEAN instability

The documentation explicitly publishes CLEAN as `Cnβ < 0` and excludes it from the first-
flight default. This is scientifically preferable to relying on flight-controller branding
or a precedent aircraft to imply stability.

### 4.2 The directional variant is reversible and coupled to packaging

V1 is an additive twin-fin module. Its planform, mass, CG, battery position, support length,
propeller clearance and drawings consume common Python-owned geometry. The repository has
also rejected earlier fin concepts when they failed structural or propeller-integration
constraints.

That is exactly the kind of whole-aircraft iteration advocated by the guide: the fin is not
treated as a decorative area pasted onto a finished airframe.

### 4.3 Static uncertainty is visible

For V1 the repository propagates independent adverse and favorable corners rather than
reporting only the nominal target:

| V1a result | `Cnβ` |
|---|---:|
| Lower corner | −0.000290 /deg |
| Nominal | +0.000500 /deg |
| Upper corner | +0.001192 /deg |

The lower corner is clearly labeled open. This audit strengthens that practice by showing
that the same band must flow into the modal and launch analyses.

### 4.4 Unmeasured slipstream benefit is not credited

The current power-on and power-off efficiency factors are both 1.00. The identical results
do not constitute a wake analysis, but they avoid making the fin smaller based on an
unmeasured pusher benefit.

### 4.5 V1 mass properties already exist

The three-dimensional equipment ledger gives configuration-specific values:

| Configuration | Mass | `Izz` |
|---|---:|---:|
| CLEAN | 1553.25 g | 0.158725 kg·m² |
| V1 | 1601.98 g | 0.168291 kg·m² |

V1 raises yaw inertia by 6.03%. The data needed to remove the CLEAN-inertia shortcut from
the V1 modal calculation is therefore already present.

### 4.6 The structural and installation risks are openly gated

The fin root, external leading-edge spar, printed saddle, support booms, propeller residual,
mass and approximate first bending mode are all marked provisional. F2 static tests,
powered clearance and E8 flight identification remain open rather than being presented as
completed physical evidence.

## 5. Quantified weaknesses in the current dynamic model

### 5.1 The nominal mode does not bound the released uncertainty

Using V1 mass and V1 inertia, the audit reproduces the following cases from the current
two-state equations:

| Speed | `Cnβ` case | Dominant behavior | Natural frequency | Damping ratio |
|---:|---|---|---:|---:|
| 45 km/h | lower | divergent, `+0.983/s` | 0.983 rad/s | −1.000 |
| 45 km/h | nominal | damped | 2.500 rad/s | 0.418 |
| 45 km/h | upper | damped | 3.790 rad/s | 0.276 |
| 60 km/h | lower | divergent, `+1.614/s` | 1.614 rad/s | −1.000 |
| 60 km/h | nominal | damped | 3.296 rad/s | 0.283 |
| 60 km/h | upper | damped | 5.029 rad/s | 0.186 |
| 95 km/h | lower | divergent, `+3.010/s` | 3.010 rad/s | −1.000 |
| 95 km/h | nominal | damped | 5.172 rad/s | 0.177 |
| 95 km/h | upper | damped | 7.932 rad/s | 0.115 |

The reduction in damping ratio as static stiffness increases is a property of this
particular incomplete two-state model, not an aircraft-level conclusion. The important
result is the change of stability class at the lower corner.

### 5.2 V1 consumes CLEAN mass properties in the released mode

`yaw_stability.yaw_inertia()` explicitly solves `reference_layout("clean")`, and the
default modal mass is also CLEAN. Using the available V1 values changes the nominal
95 km/h pair from the published `−0.952 ± 5.239i /s` to approximately
`−0.915 ± 5.091i /s`. This is not a large correction, but configuration-specific mass
properties are a basic model contract and should not be optional.

### 5.3 Fin side force is used for moments but not consistently in translation

The fin contribution is used to calculate `Cnβ` and `Cnr`. The state matrix still defaults
to generic `Cyβ = −0.15` and `Cyr = +0.25` for both CLEAN and V1. A geometry-only
diagnostic gives fin increments of approximately:

```text
Cyβ_fin = −0.536 /rad
Cyr_fin = +0.173 /rad
```

Adding only those first-order increments changes the nominal diagnostic pair from
`−0.915 ± 5.091i /s` to `−1.678 ± 5.075i /s`. That second pair is not a corrected
prediction; interference and the real section remain unknown. The large movement in decay
rate proves that the generic translational derivatives cannot be treated as harmless
constants.

### 5.4 The model cannot represent the selected polyhedral

Two degrees of polyhedral is justified as compensation for forward sweep but is not
represented in the aerodynamic solver. Therefore there is no calculated relation between
the selected value and:

- `Clβ` or spiral tendency;
- roll response after a yaw disturbance;
- sideslip-induced load redistribution;
- fin side force applied above the CG; or
- the INAV bank-to-turn control law.

The value remains a plausible starting assumption, not a closed stability decision.

## 7. Engineering test programme learned from the book

### 7.1 Gate Y0 — requirements before analysis

Define, for each CLEAN and V1 configuration:

1. approved launch method and release-speed distribution;
2. maximum crosswind, gust and release yaw/roll error;
3. required heading/roll recovery time and permitted overshoot;
4. approach, flare, touchdown and landing-area requirements;
5. acceptable control activity and actuator reserve; and
6. configuration-specific abort and loss-of-control criteria.

Without these requirements there is no objective basis for choosing a `Cnβ` target,
damping ratio, rudder or brake.

### 7.2 Gate Y1 — printed fin and junction characterization

For representative root, MAC and tip articles:

- scan the manufactured contour and leading-edge-rod transition;
- record print orientation, layer height, wall paths, density and surface treatment;
- measure or compute `CY(beta)` at the local Reynolds-number envelope;
- inspect the zero-beta deadband and repeatability;
- find separation onset and post-stall side force in both directions;
- repeat with the real root saddle/wing junction; and
- retain uncertainty on alignment, camber and left/right asymmetry.

Acceptance requires a continuous restoring slope around zero and adequate usable side
force throughout the declared finite-beta envelope. The exact numerical limits must come
from Gate Y0, not from copying a fin-volume factor from another aircraft.

### 7.3 Gate Y2 — complete-aircraft aerodynamic database

Create a database of at least:

```text
CY, Cl, Cn = f(alpha, beta, p_hat, r_hat,
               symmetric elevon, differential elevon,
               Reynolds number, throttle, configuration)
```

The database must include CLEAN and V1, measured or frozen body geometry, the real fin
section, the selected polyhedral, and uncertainty. Static `Cnβ`, damping and control
derivatives must be extracted from the same sign convention and reference geometry.

### 7.4 Gate Y3 — coupled simulation and robustness

Use the database with:

- measured full inertia tensor for each article;
- actuator rate, delay, free play and saturation;
- INAV/ArduPlane control-law configuration;
- launch hand-force and attitude distributions;
- gust/crosswind histories; and
- nonlinear stall limits.

Run Monte Carlo cases for launch, disturbance recovery, coordinated turns, slow flight,
approach and go-around/abort where applicable. V1a is acceptable only if the adverse
uncertainty cases meet the requirements established at Y0.

### 7.5 Gate Y4 — ground and captive tests

Before free flight:

1. measure mass, CG and full inertia tensor or validate it with a bifilar/trifilar test;
2. proof-load fin roots and supports;
3. measure alignment and left/right incidence;
4. survey propeller wake velocity and angularity at both fins;
5. verify propeller clearance through the complete throttle range;
6. measure fin/support modes and damping; and
7. verify sensor signs, timing, saturation and controller logs.

### 7.6 Gate Y5 — observable flight identification

Do not use the current aileron impulse as an unexplained rudder-kick substitute. A useful
programme should include:

- a calibrated, temporary and independently commanded yaw exciter, or a formally designed
  MIMO input sequence;
- synchronized airspeed, IMU, attitude, actuator command/position and throttle;
- direct sideslip measurement where practical;
- separate identification of forced response and free decay;
- repeated small and moderate amplitudes to detect nonlinearity;
- motor-off and powered cases; and
- model residuals and confidence intervals, not only a plotted decay.

The speed schedule must be written only after the longitudinal trim, flutter and active
speed-clearance P0 gates from Parts 1 and 2 are closed. Nothing in this audit authorizes a
flight or expands the current 105 km/h cap.

## 8. Acceptance logic

The following logic is recommended; numerical handling thresholds remain Gate Y0 work.

### Static directional behavior

- The lower confidence bound of `Cnβ` is stabilizing over the approved operating matrix.
- No unmodeled deadband exists around zero sideslip.
- Fin or junction separation does not create an unrecoverable yaw/roll/pitch state.

### Dynamic behavior

- Every identified lateral-directional mode is damped at the adverse mass, inertia,
  aerodynamic and controller corners.
- Model and flight data agree within declared residual and confidence limits.
- Controller stability is demonstrated with actuator rate/delay and sensor uncertainty.

### Control reserve

- Simultaneous roll command, adverse yaw, pitch trim and disturbance recovery stay inside
  approved surface travel, rate and torque.
- Recovery does not depend on a yaw effector that the aircraft does not have.
- Loss of airspeed or local fin stall cannot trap the aircraft outside its recovery basin.

### Launch and landing

- Measured release distributions stay inside the simulated recoverable set.
- A first-flight wind/yaw/roll limit and abort card are issued.
- Approach, flare, sink and touchdown requirements close with demonstrated control reserve.

## 9. Immediate repository changes recommended

1. Rename the current V1 mode result **“nominal reduced beta-r sensitivity”** rather than
   treating it as directional dynamic closure.
2. Propagate the full V1 `Cnβ` band through `yaw_modes()` and fail validation when any
   released corner diverges.
3. Make mass and inertia configuration arguments mandatory; V1 analysis must use V1.
4. Replace generic `Cyβ/Cyr` defaults with a consistent derivative manifest or make their
   estimated status and configuration explicit.
5. Define an authoritative fin section coordinate file that contains the external
   leading-edge nose and printable trailing edge.
6. Add a `lateral_directional_envelope.py` owner for the complete derivative matrix,
   uncertainty propagation and control allocation.
7. Add sideslip and differential-control capability to the span-load/stall workflow.
8. Replace the E8 aileron-kick wording with an observable identification plan.
9. Keep V1a marked **MARGINAL** and keep CLEAN out of first-flight planning.
10. Define landing and launch disturbance requirements before confirming the no-rudder,
    no-brake architecture.
11. Retire or isolate the obsolete numerical body of I-29 and the live references to
    superseded I-20 values.
12. Generate fin slope, geometry, mass and mode tables from the current calculation owner.

## 10. Part 3 conclusion

The NF Design Guide supports Salamandra's choice to treat directional stability as a
separate configuration problem and validates the benefits of a reversible fin test
article. It also shows why a vertical-tail volume calculation is only the beginning. At
model Reynolds numbers, directional behavior depends on the fin section, deadband, load
distribution, body interference, finite sideslip, damping arm, adverse yaw and stall
sequence.

The repository has a disciplined geometric and mass model, but its aerodynamic proof stops
at a small-angle static band and a nominal two-state sensitivity. The lower published V1
corner remains divergent in that same dynamic model. Until the physical fin is defined,
the complete derivative set is measured or credibly modeled, and yawed launch/landing
states are demonstrated, V1a remains a useful experimental geometry—not a flight-released
solution.
