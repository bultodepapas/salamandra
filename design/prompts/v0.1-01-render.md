# Prompt 01 — Studio Render

**Design version:** v0.1 · rev. 5 (2026-08-05) · **Purpose:** CGI product render
**Suggested size:** 3:2 or 16:9 (state it in the prompt — the model defaults to 1:1)
**Source:** [`../Salamandra-Design-Guide-v0.1.md`](../Salamandra-Design-Guide-v0.1.md)
**Technique notes:** text-only planform descriptions keep failing (the model has a strong
"flying wing = swept back" prior and misreads shape analogies as dihedral). This rev
switches to **silhouette-first generation** and **reference-image** methods.

---

## 0. What happened and why

Text descriptions of the planform keep failing:

- The model's default "flying wing" is a **delta/aft-swept** shape, and it resists
  overriding that from words alone.
- Shape analogies ("arrow pointing backward", "V", "seagull") get misread — "V" becomes
  **dihedral/gull-wing** (tips bent up), not forward sweep.
- The wing may also come out swept back, or with the sweep correct but drawn as a
  gull wing.

The fixes below stop relying on the model's interpretation of shape words:

1. **Silhouette-first (primary).** Generate a *flat top-view silhouette* first, then
   force the 3D render to copy that exact silhouette. ChatGPT keeps images generated in
   the same chat in context, so the second image follows the first one's planform.
2. **Reference image (definitive).** Upload any forward-swept aircraft photo (X-29,
   TBS Mojito, Nemesis) or a hand-drawn top-view sketch, and instruct the model to copy
   its planform.

---

## 1. Recommended workflow — silhouette first (same chat)

Run both steps in the SAME chat, one after the other.

### Step A — generate the silhouette

> Draw a simple flat top-view silhouette of an aircraft wing, black shape on white
> background, no details, no shading.
>
> The planform is a **forward-swept wing**: the two wingtips are at the TOP of the
> image and the center of the trailing edge is at the BOTTOM of the image — like an
> arrow pointing straight down. The left wingtip is at the top-left corner area, the
> right wingtip at the top-right corner area, and the center pod is at the bottom
> center. The leading edges run diagonally from the bottom center UP and OUTWARD to
> the two top corners. The trailing edge is a straight horizontal line at the bottom
> connecting to the two tips.
>
> Do not sweep the wings backward. Do not bend the wings. The wing is flat.

If Step A is wrong, iterate ONLY on Step A until the silhouette is right (it is a
simple shape, it converges quickly).

### Step B — render from the silhouette

> Use the exact wing shape of the silhouette you just drew as the planform reference.
> Keep that silhouette's outline and build the 3D model from it:
>
> **Subject.** Salamandra: a 1300 mm wingspan 3D-printed tailless flying wing (FPV
> drone) with a forward-swept planform. Build it from the silhouette, extrude the flat
> shape vertically into an airfoil section, and apply the details below:
>
> **Overall proportions.** Total wingspan 1300 mm (650 mm per half). The wing is the
> entire aircraft: there is no separate fuselage, only a small central pod. The
> wingtips are the two most forward points of the aircraft and the propeller at the
> rear center is the most rearward point. When seen from above, the outline reads as
> an arrow pointing backward — wide at the front (the tips), narrow at the rear (the
> center of the trailing edge).
>
> **Top view — planform geometry.** Tapered wing: root chord 289 mm at the centerline,
> tip chord 145 mm at each wingtip, so the tip is exactly half as wide as the root.
> The leading edge is a straight line that runs from the root outward and FORWARD,
> angling about 20° ahead of the spanwise direction; measured tip to root, the
> wingtip's leading edge sits roughly 270 mm forward of the root's quarter-chord
> point, about 200 mm ahead of the root's leading edge. The trailing edge is also a
> straight line that angles forward even more steeply than the leading edge (about
> 28° from the spanwise line), so the chord tapers smoothly and both edges sweep the
> same forward direction. The wing is widest at the centerline and narrows evenly and
> continuously toward the forward tips.
>
> **Front view.** The wing is perfectly flat spanwise: seen from the front, the
> underside is one straight horizontal line from tip to tip, and the upper surface is
> a gentle curve at the same height across the whole span. No dihedral, no raised
> tips, no gull-wing bend, no anhedral. The thickness tapers from 39 mm at the
> centerline to 13 mm at the tips.
>
> **Side view — airfoil.** The section is a smooth, lens-shaped airfoil: rounded
> leading edge, maximum thickness at about 30 % of the chord, tapering to a thin
> trailing edge with a subtle upward curl (reflex) in the last quarter of the chord.
> Both wing halves are mirror images; the aircraft is bilaterally symmetric.
>
> **Printed construction.** Fully 3D-printed in light gray PETG with visible FDM layer
> lines following the surface, a matte finish and slightly visible print seams. Each
> wing half is made of three printed segments, separated by thin recessed joint lines
> running chordwise (parallel to the centerline) at 53 % and 77 % of the half-span;
> the seams are visible on the top and bottom surfaces.
>
> **Central pod (CORE).** A short, rounded pod at the centerline covering the middle
> ~30 % of the half-span. It blends smoothly into the wing with no sharp steps: a
> rounded nose fairing that extends slightly ahead of the root leading edge, a flat
> battery hatch panel on top with a visible seam, and a smooth belly. Small FPV camera
> lens embedded in the pod nose, flush with the surface.
>
> **Propulsion.** A single electric pusher motor mounted on a short pylon at the rear
> center of the trailing edge — the rearmost point of the aircraft. It drives a black
> 8-inch (203 mm) two-blade propeller with thin swept blades and a small spinner; the
> prop disk sits behind the trailing edge, clear of the wing.
>
> **Control surfaces.** Large elevons along most of the trailing edge on both sides,
> running from about 20 % to 90 % of the half-span, separated from the wing by a thin
> hinge line at 72 % of the local chord; each elevon is about 28 % of the local chord
> wide, with a slightly beveled trailing edge and a small mass-balance horn protruding
> forward of the hinge near its inner end. No tail, no vertical stabilizer, no rudder.
>
> **Medium.** High-end 3D CGI product render.
>
> **Environment.** Seamless light gray studio background.
>
> **Composition.** Three-quarter view from the front-left, slightly elevated; the
> forward-pointing wingtips must be clearly visible ahead of the pod.
>
> **Lighting.** Soft studio lighting, diffused key light, gentle soft shadows, slight
> rim light.
>
> **Color.** Light gray dominant, neutral palette.
>
> **Mood.** Clean, precise, engineering-product showcase.
>
> **Format.** 3:2 landscape.
>
> **Exclusions.** No text, no logos, no watermark, no people, no props.
>
> **Quality.** Highly detailed, sharp focus, crisp layer-line texture.

## 2. Definitive method — reference image

If the silhouette method still fails, stop fighting the model's imagination and give it
something to copy:

1. Find any forward-swept aircraft picture: **Grumman X-29**, **TBS Mojito**, or the
   **StuntDouble Nemesis** (or draw a simple top-view sketch of the planform by hand).
2. Upload it to the chat and send:

> Use this image's WING PLANFORM as the exact reference: same silhouette, same forward
> sweep, tips ahead of the center. Build a studio render of a 3D-printed version of it:
> light gray PETG with visible FDM layer lines, matte finish, three printed segments
> per wing half with panel joints, central pod, single rear pusher motor with a black
> 8-inch two-blade propeller, large elevons on the trailing edge, no tail. Soft studio
> lighting, light gray background, three-quarter view from the front-left, 3:2
> landscape, highly detailed, no text, no logos.

The model transfers shape from uploaded images reliably (in-context learning), so this
always fixes the planform. A hand-drawn sketch works too — it does not need to be
beautiful, only top-view and unmistakably forward-swept.

---

## 3. Iteration workflow

1. Start with §1 Step A. Fix the silhouette first — do not proceed until it is right.
2. Run §1 Step B in the same chat.
3. Check the checklist. If the wing still looks swept back or gull-bent:
   - "The wing is WRONG: the wingtips must point FORWARD, ahead of the pod, and the
     wing must be FLAT. Redraw it from the silhouette."
4. If two corrections fail, switch to §2 (reference image).
5. Once right: "Rear three-quarter view showing the pusher propeller and the elevons."
6. "Show the segments and pod slightly separated along the joint lines" (exploded).

## Validation checklist (planform first)

- [ ] Wingtips AHEAD of the central pod (arrow pointing backward)
- [ ] **Wings FLAT in front view — no dihedral, no gull-wing, no raised tips**
- [ ] Propeller at the REAR center, rearmost point of the aircraft
- [ ] No tail, no vertical stabilizer
- [ ] Light gray printed texture with layer lines and segment joints
- [ ] 3:2 ratio respected
