# NF Design Guide 2024 — Salamandra Repository Audit

**Part 2: airfoil fidelity, pitch trim, static margin and elevon authority**
**Audit date:** 20 August 2026  
**Aircraft baseline:** Salamandra Article #1, release v0.6.0  
**Source:** Peter Wick, *Designing Flying Wings* (2024 English PDF), principally PDF
pages 12–23 and 31–77  
**Previous:** [Part 1 — full-book triage and release disposition](NF-Design-Guide-2024-Repository-Audit.md)

## 1. Part 2 disposition

**The released airfoil family is suitable for continued CAD investigation, but the
repository has demonstrated trim only at the 95 km/h cruise point. It has not demonstrated
longitudinal trim, stability or control reserve over the flight envelope.**

This distinction changes the maturity assessment. The current calculation verifies that
one fitted profile-moment value, +3° wash-in and the idealized elevon derivative balance at
`CL = 0.13066`. It does not verify low-speed trim near `CL = 0.5823`, Reynolds-dependent
neutral point, nonlinear elevon effectiveness, pitch damping or simultaneous pitch-and-roll
authority.

Using the repository's own low-Re fitted moments in its current linear trim equation gives
approximately **+28.7° to +37.0° symmetric elevon at 45 km/h**. This exceeds the declared
±20° mechanical envelope. A second diagnostic that evaluates section moment at the VLM
local lift coefficients still gives approximately **+27.1° to +28.2°**.

These numbers do **not** prove that the physical aircraft will require those deflections.
The book's central lesson is that low-Reynolds-number pitching moment is not constant, and
the present XFOIL evidence is too sensitive to transition, trailing-edge geometry and
extrapolation to make that claim. The numbers prove something narrower and important:

> The current model itself fails to close the low-speed trim requirement, and the missing
> physics cannot be replaced by the cruise result.

Accordingly:

| Item | Part 2 disposition |
|---|---|
| Salamandra r1 coordinates for provisional CAD | **Retain, reversible** |
| Claim that r1 closes cruise trim computationally | **Retain with narrower wording** |
| Claim that pitch authority is closed | **Withdraw** |
| 8% static-margin flight release | **Hold pending worst-case dynamic closure** |
| ±20° mechanical elevon capability | **Not an approved flight range** |
| Printed wash-in +3° | **Retain for test article only; at current structural cap** |
| First flight | **Blocked by Part 1 and Part 2 P0 findings** |

## 2. Evidence and reproducibility

The numerical evidence is generated by
[`calculations/nf_design_guide_part2_airfoil_trim.py`](../calculations/nf_design_guide_part2_airfoil_trim.py):

```bash
python3 -m pip install -r calculations/requirements.txt
python3 calculations/nf_design_guide_part2_airfoil_trim.py
```

The optional finite-trailing-edge sensitivity uses a working official XFOIL 6.99 build:

```bash
python3 calculations/nf_design_guide_part2_airfoil_trim.py \
  --xfoil /path/to/xfoil
```

The optional geometry is a symmetric linear wedge added to the released coordinates. It
is a controlled sensitivity `[I]`, not a proposed production airfoil. XFOIL output is
`[D]`; none of it becomes measured evidence `[M]`.

The current model chain is:

```text
root/tip XFOIL CM(CL), sharp trailing edge
                    │
                    ├─ fit CM at CL=0 from alpha >= 0 data
                    │
                    ▼
        c²-weighted root/tip profile CM0
                    │
                    ├─ add linear VLM wash-in moment
                    ├─ add ideal thin-airfoil/VLM elevon moment
                    ▼
        require Cm = static margin × aircraft CL
                    │
                    ▼
              one cruise trim angle
```

The physical links not yet present are measured printed contour and roughness, transition,
hinge gap, deflected-section polars, body interference, elastic twist, Reynolds-dependent
neutral point, pitch damping, actuator dynamics and controller behavior.

## 3. What the book adds to the airfoil problem

### 3.1 The aerodynamically effective airfoil is not the solid contour

The guide calls the low-Re section a “fluent airfoil.” A laminar separation bubble changes
effective camber, zero-lift angle and pitching moment with Reynolds number and angle of
attack even when the structure is perfectly rigid (PDF pp. 12–18).

This is directly applicable to Article #1:

| Condition | Root Re | Tip Re |
|---|---:|---:|
| 45 km/h | 241,026 | 120,513 |
| 95 km/h | 508,832 | 254,416 |

The factor-of-two spanwise Reynolds change means that the geometric +3° wash-in is not the
complete aerodynamic twist. Root and tip zero-lift angles can move differently with speed.
The current VLM uses geometric twist and a flat-plate lift law; it does not ingest the
XFOIL `CL(alpha, Re)` curves. Therefore the calculated span load and trim do not yet
contain the book's fluent-airfoil mechanism.

### 3.2 Pitching moment is the sensitive quantity for a tailless aircraft

The book gives examples where XFOIL's low-angle moment prediction disagrees qualitatively
with flown aircraft. It attributes the problem to laminar-bubble and trailing-edge pressure
behavior and cautions against treating the quarter-chord moment as constant at model
Reynolds numbers (PDF pp. 16–23).

Salamandra is especially exposed because:

- there is no tailplane to absorb section-moment error;
- the root/tip moment is deliberately used to set wash-in and neutral elevon;
- +3° wash-in is already at the declared structural cap;
- the cruise trim cap is only ±0.6°; and
- `Delta Cm = 0.001` changes calculated trim by **0.547°**.

Thus, an apparently small moment-coefficient error is already comparable to the entire
cruise trim acceptance band.

### 3.3 Realistic trailing-edge geometry is an aerodynamic input

The guide reports that a finite trailing edge smooths the calculated moment curve and
recommends a 0.1%-chord XFOIL sensitivity for molded flying-wing airfoils (PDF pp.
20–22). It also suggests inspecting moment near 4–5° angle of attack, trying lower Ncrit
and optionally tripping the lower surface near 95% chord.

These are empirical diagnostics, not universal settings. Salamandra should not simply
change Ncrit to 6 or set every CAD trailing edge to 0.1% chord. The relevant geometric
comparison is:

| Geometry | Root | Tip |
|---|---:|---:|
| Guide sensitivity | 0.1% c = 0.289 mm | 0.1% c = 0.145 mm |
| One declared 0.45 mm print line | 0.156% c | 0.311% c |

The likely printed tip edge is therefore more than three times the guide's fractional
example. Actual nozzle path, bevel, sanding and shrinkage must be measured rather than
represented by the sharp CAD points.

### 3.4 An airfoil family must be optimized over its mission, not one polar

The guide treats a spanwise airfoil family as a mission-specific design. Each member must
retain useful drag, lift, zero-lift angle, moment and stall behavior at its local Reynolds
number, including the intermediate blends (PDF pp. 34–47). It warns that aggressive
strakes can create unintended aerodynamic twist.

The repository has correctly generated nine compatible geometric stations and uses exact
root/tip `c²` moment weights. However, only the root and tip have stored r1 polars. The
seven intermediate sections are assumed to interpolate aerodynamically because their
geometry interpolates. The guide provides no basis for that assumption; laminar-bubble
behavior can be nonlinear with geometry and Reynolds number.

### 3.5 Small static margin is a performance tool, not a substitute for stability proof

The guide shows why lower static margin reduces trim deflection and effective-polar loss,
especially when elevator span is limited (PDF pp. 48–54). It then gives the essential
constraint: a stabilizing restoring moment must remain in the worst flight condition, with
precise and play-free controls (PDF pp. 54–56).

The correct Salamandra interpretation is therefore not “reduce static margin until trim
closes.” It is:

1. determine the real neutral-point range over Reynolds number, control deflection and
   configuration;
2. model pitch inertia, damping, actuator and controller dynamics;
3. establish the minimum acceptable restoring/damping margin; and
4. only then select CG for the lowest trim drag consistent with robust stability.

### 3.6 Longitudinal inertia belongs in the stability model

The guide connects longitudinal mass distribution to the minimum flyable static margin
and notes that the relationship is not completely captured by simple static rules (PDF
pp. 63–64).

The repository already contains the necessary mass-property starting point:

| Configuration | `Iyy` | Pitch radius of gyration |
|---|---:|---:|
| CLEAN | 0.061089 kg·m² | 198.3 mm |
| V1 | 0.070603 kg·m² | 209.9 mm |

V1 raises the modeled pitch inertia approximately 15.6%. Yet neither inertia appears in
a longitudinal mode, handling-quality or control-loop robustness analysis. The book's
lesson is therefore actionable without importing any empirical static-margin percentage:
use the mass model already present in the repository.

### 3.7 A flying-wing airfoil is always a flap airfoil

The guide stresses that airfoil performance must be evaluated with the deflections needed
to fly, not only with the flap in its geometric zero position. It reports a broad optimum
near 25% flap chord, useful behavior over approximately 15–40%, and increasingly nonlinear
moment response beyond roughly 10–20° at low Reynolds number (PDF pp. 48–51 and 68–70).

Salamandra's 28% chord is a sound starting point. Its current effectiveness
`tau = 0.6408` and `Cm_delta = +0.001828/physical degree` are ideal potential-flow
results. They omit precisely the effects the book says govern real flap behavior:
separation, gap leakage, hysteresis, surface accuracy and changing section moment.

## 4. What the repository already does well

### 4.1 The corrected r1 generation pipeline is technically disciplined

The current generator improves materially on the superseded B3 work:

- thickness changes about the mean camber line rather than scaling every ordinate;
- the polar cache is keyed to geometry and analysis settings;
- root and tip use their actual 45/95 km/h Reynolds envelope;
- root/tip moment is integrated with exact `c²` weights, 0.607143/0.392857;
- post-stall points are excluded from the fitted intercept; and
- every generated station is checked for the requested thickness.

These corrections match the guide's demand for a coherent airfoil family rather than an
arbitrary root section and a thinned tip.

### 4.2 The repository does not trust XFOIL stall as the aircraft stall value

Stored r1 XFOIL polars predict endpoint section `clmax` of at least 1.076, but the design
uses a much lower generic measured section screen of 0.65 and derives wing
`CLmax = 0.589`. This is appropriately conservative as a preliminary calculation.

It is still not a physical closure: the 0.65 datum is not a measurement of the printed r1
sections, and the map from a generic section limit to the three-dimensional wing does not
include hinge gaps, roughness, sideslip or elastic twist.

### 4.3 The elevon geometry is well chosen for a first test article

The selected surface retains 94.5% of the previous linear roll derivative, removes moving
area at the removable joint, preserves a fixed tip, reduces the hinge proxy and keeps the
chord inside the guide's effective range. Those are defensible multidisciplinary reasons.

The decision is a geometry selection, not a flight-authority validation. The repository
mostly says this correctly in I-27 and ADR-0045.

## 5. Quantitative moment audit

### 5.1 Stored sharp-edge polars

The released polars begin at `alpha = 0°`; no negative-alpha points are stored. Therefore
`Cm0` at `CL = 0` is an extrapolation whenever the first point has positive lift. The
problem is most pronounced at the low-Re tip:

| Station / condition | Ncrit | Minimum stored `CL` | Fitted `Cm0` | `Cm` at 4.5° |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Root, 45 km/h | 10 | +0.0661 | +0.00910 | −0.00860 |
| Root, 45 km/h | 12 | +0.0829 | +0.00292 | −0.00900 |
| Root, 95 km/h | 10 | +0.0553 | +0.01602 | −0.00960 |
| Root, 95 km/h | 12 | +0.0513 | +0.01689 | −0.00960 |
| Tip, 45 km/h | 10 | +0.2205 | −0.04780 | −0.00800 |
| Tip, 45 km/h | 12 | +0.2655 | −0.07687 | −0.01010 |
| Tip, 95 km/h | 10 | +0.1524 | −0.01646 | −0.00490 |
| Tip, 95 km/h | 12 | +0.1621 | −0.02077 | −0.00510 |

At the low-Re tip, the fitted intercept is extrapolated from data beginning at
`CL = 0.2205–0.2655`. The Ncrit-12 fitted value and the 4.5° value differ by **0.0668**.
That is not a usable uncertainty band; it is evidence that a single constant `Cm0` is an
inadequate representation of this polar.

### 5.2 Integrated profile moments

| Condition | Ncrit | Current fitted `Cm0` | Integrated `Cm` at 4.5° | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 45 km/h endpoints | 10 | −0.01325 | −0.00836 | +0.00489 |
| 45 km/h endpoints | 12 | −0.02842 | −0.00943 | +0.01899 |
| 95 km/h endpoints | 10 | +0.00326 | −0.00775 | −0.01101 |
| 95 km/h endpoints | 12 | +0.00209 | −0.00783 | −0.00993 |

The 4.5° diagnostic should not be substituted directly into the wing trim equation: root
and tip do not operate at one common angle of attack, and the book's recommendation is an
empirical estimate for non-extreme plank sections. The table is useful because two
reasonable interpretations change integrated moment by 0.005–0.019, while only 0.001
changes cruise trim by 0.547°.

### 5.3 Finite-trailing-edge XFOIL sensitivity

The optional Part 2 run used XFOIL 6.99 at the root/tip cruise Reynolds numbers. The
following table gives the calculated moment at 4.5°:

| Edge sensitivity | Ncrit | Root `Cm(4.5°)` | Tip `Cm(4.5°)` | `c²`-integrated |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Sharp | 6 | −0.0050 | −0.0052 | −0.00508 |
| Sharp | 10 | −0.0104 | −0.0053 | −0.00840 |
| Sharp | 12 | −0.0104 | −0.0054 | −0.00844 |
| 0.1% c linear wedge | 6 | −0.0031 | −0.0023 | −0.00279 |
| 0.1% c linear wedge | 10 | −0.0028 | −0.0023 | −0.00260 |
| 0.1% c linear wedge | 12 | −0.0028 | −0.0024 | −0.00264 |
| One 0.45 mm line | 6 | −0.0024 | −0.0012 | −0.00193 |
| One 0.45 mm line | 10 | −0.0021 | −0.0009 | −0.00163 |
| One 0.45 mm line | 12 | −0.0021 | −0.0011 | −0.00171 |

The calculation reproduces the guide's qualitative observation: finite trailing-edge
geometry substantially smooths and shifts the moment result. It also shows that the
aircraft-specific printable edge matters more than choosing Ncrit 10 versus 12 at this
4.5° diagnostic point.

The result remains a geometry sensitivity, because a linear wedge changes the complete
aft pressure recovery and is not the same as a printed bevel or blunt edge. It justifies
measuring and analyzing the actual contour before changing the released airfoil.

## 6. The missing trim envelope

The current linear trim equation is:

```text
delta_e = [SM × CL - (Cm_profile + Cm_twist)] / Cm_delta_e
```

with:

| Quantity | Current value |
|---|---:|
| Static margin | 0.08 |
| Wash-in | +3.0° |
| `dCm/dtwist` | +0.002468/deg |
| Wash-in contribution | +0.007405 |
| `dCm/delta_e` | +0.001828/physical deg |
| Positive elevon sign | trailing-edge-up/reflex |

Applying this equation to the fitted moments, interpolated linearly with speed, gives:

| Speed | Aircraft `CL` | Trim, Ncrit 10 | Trim, Ncrit 12 | ±20° mechanical check |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 45 km/h | 0.58230 | **+28.68°** | **+36.98°** | **Fail** |
| 60 km/h | 0.32755 | +14.82° | **+20.82°** | Fail at adverse case |
| 75 km/h | 0.20963 | +6.95° | +10.65° | Pass mechanically |
| 95 km/h | 0.13066 | −0.12° | +0.52° | Pass cruise CAD cap |

This screen contains simplifying assumptions: constant fitted profile moment at each
Reynolds endpoint, ideal linear elevon effect and no feedback of deflection into the
section polar or lift distribution. The correct response is not to trust the exact angles.
It is to recognize that the repository never evaluated its trim equation away from cruise.

A first-order endpoint interpolation of `Cm(CL)` at the actual VLM local lift
coefficients gives +27.13/+28.19° at 45 km/h. Only 0.5–0.8% of the `c²` weight requires
CL extrapolation at that condition. At 95 km/h the same diagnostic requires 100%
extrapolation because the stored tip polars begin above the calculated outer-wing local
`CL`; it therefore cannot replace new polar runs.

Three coupled consequences follow:

1. The +3° wash-in decision may close cruise while failing the low-speed design point.
2. A large symmetric trailing-edge-up command consumes the same physical travel needed
   for differential roll control during launch and stall recovery.
3. The book reports increasing flap nonlinearity beyond approximately 10–20°, exactly
   where this diagnostic enters the mechanical limit.

The existing statement that 5° provides roughly ten times the trim residual is true only
for the 95 km/h residual. It must not be presented as whole-envelope control reserve.

## 7. Static margin, CG and neutral-point uncertainty

### 7.1 Current numerical scales

| Quantity | Absolute | Equivalent fraction of MAC |
|---|---:|---:|
| Nominal static margin | 17.997 mm | 8.000% |
| VLM–Weissinger NP spread | 2.889 mm | 1.284% |
| Released CG half-band | 5.000 mm | 2.223% |
| CLEAN one-sigma x-CG uncertainty | 7.810 mm | 3.472% |

The one-sigma CG uncertainty alone maps the nominal 8% static margin to approximately
4.53–11.47% before neutral-point, body, viscous or elastic uncertainty is included. The
input distributions are not proven normal, so a three-sigma probability claim would be
inappropriate; the comparison simply shows that the declared uncertainty is larger than
the accepted physical band.

The book cites possible airfoil-level neutral-point movements of order ±5% chord in low-Re
examples (PDF pp. 54–56). That is not directly transferable to the complete swept wing,
but it invalidates any assumption that the 1.284% inviscid method spread bounds the real
aircraft neutral point.

### 7.2 Moment error and CG equivalence

At the cruise `CL`, a profile-moment error of 0.001 is equivalent to:

- 0.547° calculated elevon trim;
- 0.765% MAC static margin; or
- 1.722 mm CG movement.

The root/tip Ncrit and trailing-edge sensitivities are several times larger. Therefore a
±5 mm balance tolerance is not meaningful unless aerodynamic-model uncertainty is kept
separate and the actual flight neutral point is identified.

### 7.3 Static stability is not dynamic stability

The current NP/CG calculation gives `Cm_alpha < 0` in the inviscid rigid model by
construction. It does not calculate the short-period or phugoid modes, `Cm_q`, actuator
delay, airspeed-loop coupling or pitch-controller robustness. The existing `Iyy` model is
not connected to the aerodynamic derivatives.

Part 2 therefore rejects both easy fixes:

- moving CG aft to reduce trim without proving the worst-case restoring moment; and
- moving CG forward to mask uncertain moment behavior while consuming elevon authority
  and increasing drag.

## 9. Required Part 2 engineering programme

### 9.1 Geometry and process characterization

Produce root, midspan and tip coupons using the intended printer, orientation, material,
wall paths and finishing process. For each coupon measure:

- chord and thickness distribution;
- upper/lower ordinates and twist;
- trailing-edge thickness and bevel;
- surface waviness and roughness;
- hinge gap/step and neutral alignment; and
- repeatability across at least three prints.

Store the scans as measured coordinate sets with uncertainty, not as replacements for the
nominal CAD files.

### 9.2 Section aerodynamic matrix

At minimum, analyze and where possible measure:

| Variable | Required cases |
|---|---|
| Reynolds number | 120k, 240k, 255k and 510k, plus local intermediate-station values |
| Section | root, representative midspan and tip |
| Geometry | nominal CAD, measured mean print and adverse measured print |
| Transition | Ncrit 6/10/12 sensitivity; free and declared upper/lower trips |
| Angle of attack | negative branch through zero lift, cruise range, high lift and stall |
| Elevon | enough positive/negative angles to cover proposed trim, roll and recovery commands |
| Outputs | `CL`, `CD`, `Cm_c/4`, transition, separation, hysteresis and convergence status |

The purpose of Ncrit 6 is sensitivity to the guide's empirical recommendation, not a new
calibration. Drag calibration and moment validation must remain separate.

### 9.3 Coupled wing trim model

Create a new calculation that:

1. assigns each span station its local geometry, Reynolds number and section database;
2. solves the three-dimensional lift distribution with section lift-curve variation;
3. integrates `Cm(CL, Re, delta_e)` with `c²` weighting;
4. iterates aircraft angle of attack, elastic twist and elevon to force/moment equilibrium;
5. combines symmetric trim and differential roll commands without exceeding actuator,
   hinge-moment or structural limits; and
6. reports every interpolation and extrapolation fraction.

Required output points include maximum mass near stall, launch, climb, routine cruise,
maximum initial speed, power-on/off and the full CG tolerance range.

### 9.4 Longitudinal dynamics

Connect the existing `Iyy` and mass model to a longitudinal state-space analysis. The model
must include at least `CL_alpha`, `Cm_alpha`, `Cm_q`, `Cm_delta`, actuator bandwidth/delay,
airspeed measurement and the active pitch loop. Evaluate both CLEAN and V1 because their
pitch inertia differs.

The acceptance question is not a handbook static-margin percentage. It is whether every
declared configuration retains restoring tendency, adequate damping, controllability and
controller robustness at the unfavorable uncertainty corner.

### 9.5 Physical closure

A complete-aircraft glide/trim test can infer the integrated result but cannot uniquely
identify root versus tip section behavior. The preferred sequence is:

1. printed-section or representative-panel force/moment testing;
2. ground measurement of control free play and load-deflection;
3. article mass, CG and inertia measurement;
4. low-risk flight identification of NP and trim versus dynamic pressure; and
5. progressive expansion only inside the aeroelastic clearance established in Part 1.

## 10. Proposed acceptance criteria for review

The exact numerical margins require the new model and physical data. The review logic
should nevertheless be fixed now:

- **Stability:** positive restoring tendency and adequate damping at every mass/CG/Re/
  configuration corner, not merely at nominal cruise.
- **Trim:** equilibrium achievable without entering a region where measured flap
  effectiveness collapses or becomes hysteretic.
- **Control reserve:** simultaneous pitch trim, roll command and disturbance recovery fit
  inside the approved travel and torque envelope with explicit reserve.
- **Performance:** routine-flight trim preferably remains in the guide's small-deflection
  regime; the ±20° mechanical stop is never treated as a performance target.
- **Stall:** separation progression retains pitch and roll recovery authority on the
  printed wing.
- **Balance:** measured article CG lies inside the released band, and analytical uncertainty
  is not used as a substitute for weighing and balancing the aircraft.
- **Traceability:** every accepted polar identifies geometry, print/process, Re, transition,
  surface deflection, solver/test setup and uncertainty.

## 11. Immediate repository changes recommended

1. Rename the current result from “elevon authority/trim closure” to **“95 km/h cruise
   trim screen.”**
2. Add negative-alpha, finite-edge and deflected-surface cases to the r1 XFOIL workflow.
3. Derive the `CM0_WING_N10/N12` values from a polar manifest instead of copying constants
   into `elevon_sizing.py`.
4. Add a `longitudinal_trim_envelope.py` owner that fails if any required speed/mass/CG
   case exceeds approved control authority.
5. Keep +3° wash-in reversible until the new envelope and printed-section tests pass.
6. Prevent any aft-CG/static-margin reduction intended only to make the current trim
   equation pass.
7. Update I-10, I-15, I-27, ADR-0041 and the concise/advanced guides to distinguish
   cruise trim, mechanical travel and flight-approved travel.
8. Add landing and flare to the aircraft requirements before deciding that two elevons
   with no brake mode are sufficient.

## 12. Part 2 conclusion

The NF Design Guide validates several aspects of the repository's engineering approach:
the airfoil must be designed as a family, realistic Reynolds numbers matter, a 28%-chord
elevon is plausible and small trim angles are valuable. Its deeper lesson overturns the
present sense of closure: at model Reynolds numbers, the quantities used to prove trim are
the least constant and least trustworthy parts of the polar.

Salamandra r1 should remain the provisional test geometry because replacing it without
measured evidence would only exchange one model for another. The next step is to measure
the printed geometry, build a full `Cm(CL, Re, delta)` database, couple it to the wing and
longitudinal dynamics, and demonstrate control reserve from launch/stall through cruise.
